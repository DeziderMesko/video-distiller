# Summary: LDO Miley V2.0 (full production kit) Ep1 #millenniummachines #opensource #cnc

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM

---

## TL;DR
This is a multi-hour livestream from the Millennium Machines project (makers of the Milo/Miley CNC mill) in which the host begins building the new Milo V2 kit's Z-axis using LDO Motors' official hardware and a manual written by teammate Bright. Interwoven with the build are long stretches of chat banter (dogs, cats, trade-show gossip, filament chat) and a guest call with Alan from West3D (an LDO/Voron reseller and fellow Millennium dev). The build content itself is useful if you're assembling this exact kit — anti-backlash nut preload, insert sizes, fastener specs — but it's a small fraction of the runtime.

## Key points
- First-ever build of Milo/Miley following Bright's official written manual rather than the host's own memory; order starts with the Z-axis [7:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=438s), [7:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=460s).
- LDO's kit ships Voron-spec M3 heat inserts and the lead-screw (not ball-screw) hardware version; ball-screw only makes sense if you're using servos instead of steppers [10:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=612s), [10:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=647s).
- Host has been feeding issues back to LDO's Jason: the mainboard (Sila) uses ferrules/bare wire instead of JST connectors on most channels, so builders will need to cut off JSTs and strip wire for the screw terminals [12:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=742s).
- Detailed anti-backlash nut assembly: opposing Belleville (conical) compression washers preload the lead-screw nut against the block to remove backlash; LDO supplies a grub screw to seal the new grease port and threaded posts in place of loose pins [30:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=1841s), [38:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=2325s).
- Preload got it right on the first attempt; a decent field check is spinning the assembly with a drill on torque setting 1–5 — if it doesn't spin at low torque but does near 5, preload is in the right ballpark [45:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=2730s), [47:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=2846s).
- LDO's manufacturing has already improved on the original design: the compression plate went from 4 to 8 mounting holes (to work around lead-screw-start/hole misalignment) and added 0.5 mm M8 shims for finer preload adjustment — LDO effectively contributing design fixes back to Millennium [2:20:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=8402s).
- LDO kit pricing is roughly known but pre-order timing is still unconfirmed (hoped-for "end of this month," per LDO contact Jose, but dates have slipped before and aren't final) [2:16:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=8203s).
- Guest Alan (West3D) discusses testing Fiberon (Polymaker) engineering filaments: PPS-CF has an HDT around 260°C, PPA-CF around 220°C but with much higher strength; glass-fiber blends damp vibration roughly 2x better than equivalent carbon-fiber blends [2:29:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=8949s), [2:29:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=8980s), [2:30:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=9005s), [2:32:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=9136s).
- Host vents about Bambu Lab's ("Bamboo") slicer/connectivity friction and explains why they moved to a Prusa Core One printer instead [2:44:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=9875s).
- General industry take: 3D-printed structural machine parts are increasingly being replaced by CNC/milled parts as designs mature (cites a poorly executed third-party milled Voron kit that added pointless "speed holes," hurting mass/vibration damping while raising cost) [3:09:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=11379s).
- Host is reconsidering exhibiting at their main UK show ("Smurf") after a bad booth placement last year despite paying about £1,000 for a silver membership, with venue space shrinking again this year [2:47:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=10038s), [2:48:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=10084s).

## Details worth keeping
- Fastener conventions for this build: anything into a T-nut is M5x10; anything into the end of an extrusion is M5x12 [1:04:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=3853s).
- Ballast boxes are filled with epoxy granite (epoxy + sand) to damp frame vibration — only fill non-moving frame sections, not moving axes, to avoid adding mass that causes skipped steps [1:40:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=6003s).
- No brand-name Loctite on hand during the stream — host substitutes finger-tight assembly and plans to buy actual threadlocker before the next session [38:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=2325s).
- West3D (Portland-based LDO/Voron retailer) keeps a small "Voron museum" including Phoenix serial #1, Switchwire serial #0, and a Millennium Machines V2 auctioned at a past Smurf show [1:49:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A5sJitOpaNM&t=6555s).

## Skip it if
You want a tight, edited build tutorial — this is an unedited stream where the actual hands-on assembly is frequently interrupted by 20+ minutes of chat tangents, guest chit-chat, and unrelated hobby talk. Also skip if you're not specifically building the LDO Milo/Miley V2 CNC kit, since most of the technical value is kit-specific.
