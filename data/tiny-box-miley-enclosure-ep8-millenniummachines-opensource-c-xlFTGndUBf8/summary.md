# Summary: Tiny Box Miley Enclosure Ep8 #millenniummachines #opensource #cnc

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8

---

## TL;DR
This is a livestream by the Millennium Machines team (creators of the open-source Milo/Miley/Atlas CNC machines) doing final assembly on the Tiny Box enclosure prototype — wiring, RGB status lights, and a front-panel swap — before moving into a full unboxing and quality review of the early-access "Miley" hardware kit from manufacturer LDO. Along the way the host chats with team members Alan and Rosh (joining via Discord) about a CAN-bus expansion board concept, tool-setter/four-pack design tradeoffs, and pendant/UI hardware options, plus general 3D-printer industry gossip (Bambu Lab fire concerns, tool-changer printers, Voron pricing). It's a casual, chatty build/community stream rather than a tutorial — useful mainly for people following this specific project's development.

## Key points
- Tiny Box enclosure reaches near-final assembly on stream: new front panel (with hinge notches) installed, internal wiring (end-stop loop, power supplies, VFD) done off-screen, RGB status LEDs wired (red = unsafe/e-stop state, white = normal, custom colors planned for tool changes/homing in the next MOS version) [5:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=329s), [6:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=404s), [13:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=790s).
- Host does a full unboxing of the "Miley" early-access hardware kit from LDO: includes collets (3mm/6mm), gauge pins, shim washers, heat-insert tips, dial indicators, a 1.5kW ER16 220V spindle (now black powder-coated, centered input, confirmed grounded via multimeter test), cable chains, TR8 lead screws, NEMA 23 motors, an LDO-branded apron and 3M safety glasses, CNC'd AB blocks, split-collar lead-screw collars (an upgrade over V1.5's grub-screw collars), thrust bearings replacing most 608 bearings, a 3D touch probe, and stainless steel rails [1:32:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=5532s)–[2:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=7200s).
- Pricing estimate given: Miley kit ~CAD 2,180 (~£1,200/$1,600) plus the Universal Electronics Box (UEB) at ~CAD 625, totaling roughly £1,500 — about £200 more than the old V1.5 kit despite adding CNC'd parts and electronics, and cheaper than a comparable Voron Cascade (~$3,000 base) [2:03:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=7382s)–[2:04:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=7464s).
- Host says he's now willing to endorse the LDO kit again after an earlier rocky period where feedback "fell on deaf ears" — quality and finish are now "immaculate" and prior V1.5 problems have been fixed [2:00:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=7254s)–[2:01:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=7308s).
- Design rationale: Miley (moving-platform design) is expected to become more popular than Atlas because it keeps a smaller footprint and lower enclosure cost (acrylic/polycarbonate is the dominant cost driver, ~£500 of the total) [14:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=879s)–[15:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=913s).
- Team discusses (with Alan) an idea for a general-purpose CAN expansion board built on the Sammy 32-pin board, to add software-controllable thermistor pull-ups/downs (3-4 thermistor inputs: ambient, machine/extrusion temperature for thermal-expansion compensation, and electronics monitoring) plus spare digital I/O for relays/solenoids, since all pins on the Scylla control board are already in use [26:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=1563s)–[32:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=1947s).
- Host explains why a tool setter won't be built into the "four pack" tool rack: it would force everyone into one tool-setter type, there's no physical space without giving up a tool bay, and maintaining a niche variant isn't worth the design hours for an estimated ~10 interested users [34:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=2041s)–[38:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=2316s).
- Pendant/UI hardware discussion: Furky's Raspberry Pi + 7" touchscreen pendant (shown at SMRRF and Rocky Mountain RepRap Festival) sends encoder pulses to the Scylla board via Ethernet and runs Duet Web Control; a simpler "PanelDue"-based version is planned but would be RepRapFirmware-only; host dislikes tablet-based UIs (e.g. Amazon Fire tablets) due to bricking risk and short security-update lifespans [49:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=2951s)–[1:01:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=3667s).
- Community/industry side-chat: concern over Bambu Lab A1/A1 mini/A2L bed-heater cable fire reports; host argues Bambu's "A series" targets low-end trinket printing rather than serious use, and that no major printer maker has solved cable strain relief despite it being a known issue since the Anet A8 [1:09:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=4173s)–[1:10:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=4236s); also discussed unenclosed CoreXY tool-changer printers (U1, Creality's oddly-named model, Flashforge, Sovol) as unsuitable for ABS due to poor chamber heating, noting Bambu chambers only reach ~65°C passively with no dedicated heater [1:13:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=4412s)–[1:15:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=4504s).
- Host reveals his garage was broken into and touchscreen/Duet boards, end mills, and printer parts were stolen; end mills are especially costly to replace given current carbide prices [1:02:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=3720s)–[1:04:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=3864s).

## Details worth keeping
- Miley kit pricing: ~CAD 2,180 for the machine, ~CAD 625 for the UEB; total roughly £1,500 (~$1,600–1,800 USD), pre-orders expected end of the month, beta/alpha release targeted in 1-2 weeks [2:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=7200s)–[2:04:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=7464s).
- Design constraint: all Millennium Machines printed parts are sized to fit a 250mm build plate (Voron Trident/2.4, Bambu-class printers) [1:06:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=4018s)–[1:07:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=4033s).
- Spindle is now grounded (confirmed via multimeter continuity test on one pin to chassis) — earlier spindle batches reportedly were not grounded [1:41:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=6063s)–[1:42:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=6144s).
- Recommended: use the CNC'd AB blocks over printed ones for rigidity/longevity, and check that HDZ shrouds vs. HIWIN-style rails are chosen based on availability/aesthetics [1:46:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=6360s)–[1:46:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=6394s).
- Blind-joint Z-column mod exists (adds rigidity by tramming off the Z base plate instead of the column) — requires 8 extra holes and M5x10 bolts, not included in the main manual because it complicates tramming for beginners [2:05:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=7517s)–[2:06:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=7586s).
- Filament note: parts printed in Polymaker's polycarbonate/carbon-fiber blend showed zero warping, while PETG-GF warped more than the polycarbonate [2:11:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=7914s)–[2:12:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=7938s).
- Reference: N17 CAN expansion board (linked in stream chat) from "Nine Mile" adds a stepper driver and end-stop/aux I/O over CAN for driving accessories like the four-pack tool rack [44:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=2681s)–[46:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlFTGndUBf8&t=2812s).
