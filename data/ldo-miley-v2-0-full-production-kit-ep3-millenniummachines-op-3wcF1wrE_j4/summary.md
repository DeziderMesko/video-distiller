# Summary: LDO Miley V2.0 (full production kit) Ep3 #millenniummachines #opensource #cnc

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4

---

## TL;DR
A short, mostly-improvised Millennium Machines livestream (about 1h27m, much shorter than usual) in which the host continues assembling the LDO-produced Milo CNC kit — finishing the Y-axis motor mount and rail alignment and starting the Z-axis — while fielding chat questions about lubricants, upgrading printed parts to metal, and progress on MillenniumOS (MOS), the project's control software. There's a fair amount of banter (weather, food, a teammate's driver-killing mishap) but the technical content is genuinely useful if you're building this exact kit or following MOS/NXT development. The host makes and fixes a couple of assembly mistakes live (missing drive pin, reversed Z-axis screw), which doubles as a warning for other builders.

## Key points
- MOS (MillenniumOS) development update: adding a UI ops page for drilling/facing/stock-squaring, configurable RGB status lighting (idle/homing/tool-change states), the ability to re-run individual wizard sections instead of the whole setup wizard, and integrating teammate Cade's NXT tool-table work into current MOS; NXT itself (the next-gen rewrite) is still some way off [11:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=695s).
- LDO kit quality check-in: the extrusion hole-drilling issue that had worried the host has been resolved, and the kit is otherwise up to good standards, though the release date is expected to slip past what LDO originally indicated [15:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=903s).
- The kit's shaft locking collars need much more torque than expected before they actually compress — host nearly strips one on camera before realizing he was turning it the wrong way [20:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=1201s).
- Repeated, hard-learned warning: the drive pin in the AB (anti-backlash) block/lead-screw assembly is critical — skip it and you risk skewing the drive nut, since it's designed with built-in adjustment clearance. Host forgets it twice on stream [36:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=2210s), [1:00:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=3616s).
- Guidance on which parts benefit from a metal/CNC upgrade vs. staying 3D printed: motor and bearing mounts are compression-loaded and print well; anything bending-loaded (side plates, XY plates, the frame) prints poorly and is a bad upgrade target. Best ROI upgrade is the X motor-mount/bearing block, since it also acts as a torsional stiffener for the bed [55:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=3303s), [1:01:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=3679s).
- Fastener rule for this kit's CNC-machined parts: M3x10 screws go into machined blocks, versus M3x40 on the equivalent 3D-printed part (needed there to pull the heat-insert through rather than just pull out) [40:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=2402s).
- Cautionary tale about a third party that CNC-milled a full set of the printed V1.5 parts but added pointless "speed holes" — a 3D-printing optimization (less mass = faster printing) that on CNC just means paying more machining time to remove material, hurting both cost and mass/vibration damping [1:02:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=3770s), [1:04:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=3850s).
- Host's own irony: he follows other people's build manuals carefully but keeps deviating from teammate Bright's manual on his own machine, causing the Z-axis screw/AB block to go in backwards and needing to be pulled apart and reflipped [1:02:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=3770s), [1:07:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=4025s).
- Plan for the build: next session covers the X-axis (built as one single piece), then one or two more sessions finish the physical assembly before actual test cuts; host may swap the LDO test motors for Ooznest ones over wiring-length concerns once the enclosure is built [1:26:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=5162s).

## Details worth keeping
- Rail alignment procedure demonstrated step by step: reference one rail against a 1-2-3 block, loosen all carriage bolts except the far end one, drive the carriage back and forth checking for binding by feel, then tighten two bolts per carriage at a time working down the rail before final-tightening the rail itself [44:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=2699s)–[52:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=3176s).
- Cutting-fluid/cleaning fluid options for aluminum: IPA, WD-40, and kerosene-based fluids all work; all are flammable, IPA is more of a respiratory irritant, and none should be used indoors without ventilation. Alcohol- and WD-40-based fluids strip grease from the machine over time, so rails need regular reoiling/regreasing [28:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=1721s).
- Maintenance recommendation: grease the machine at least monthly, or oil once a day (or right before use) if relying on oil instead — oil is the technically superior lubricant but less convenient [35:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=2101s).
- Never flood-coolant with anything flammable (alcohol/kerosene-based fluids); mist systems are fine [28:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=1721s).
- Host is checking West3D's listings daily for a firm LDO Milo release/pricing update [15:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=903s).
- Community note: teammate Hans has gone quiet on his build due to personal issues — cited as the reason the host keeps a strict bar for who joins the dev team, since burnout/life getting in the way is common in open-source projects [28:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3wcF1wrE_j4&t=1721s).
