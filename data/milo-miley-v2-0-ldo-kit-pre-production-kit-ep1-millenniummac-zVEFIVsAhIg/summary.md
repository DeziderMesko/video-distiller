# Summary: Milo/Miley V2.0 LDO Kit Pre production kit Ep1 #millenniummachines #opensource  #cncmachine

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg

---

## TL;DR
A long, casual live build-stream from the Milo/Miley (LDO Motion Systems V2 CNC router) project, in which the host and a guest, Hans, start assembling the Y-axis on their respective pre-production V2 kits (Milo and Miley variants). Roughly two-thirds of the stream is community chatter, Discord/audio troubleshooting, and dog/cat interludes, but interspersed are genuinely useful build notes: known pre-production defects (wrong heat inserts), the new trammable joint design, machine dimensions, Z-travel limits, coolant guidance, and previews of an upcoming vise and the larger "Atlas" machine. It's an informal hangout stream, not a structured tutorial — useful mainly to people already building or planning to build a Milo/Miley V2.

## Key points
- This is a pre-production kit: the heat inserts shipped are the wrong (old V1.5/CNC Kitchen) size and too small for the Voron-spec bores the parts are designed around; LDO is aware, and CNC Kitchen-spec printed parts still work as a fallback — check your kit before printing anything [13:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=821s) [14:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=879s).
- New V2 tramming joint (Y/Z) was specifically designed to fix a usability problem: V1.5's shim-based tramming was fiddly for newcomers; the V2 joint lets you separate the Z-axis and tilt/shim it far more easily, at a small cost to raw mechanical stiffness [1:11:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=4286s) [1:12:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=4320s).
- Machine dimensions: Milo work area is 350mm (X) / 200mm (Y), Miley is 310mm (X) / 200mm (Y); V1.5 was 340×160mm; Z travel is 120mm usable on both (Miley shows 110mm nominally because its fixture plate is built in) [1:21:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=4865s) [1:21:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=4905s).
- Z-travel is deliberately capped around 120mm by design — a "shoulder" on the Z-plate is required to get usable Y clearance, and extending rail length doesn't help since the shoulder height itself is the limiting factor; the designer says 120mm is more than enough for virtually any real job [1:59:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=7162s) [2:00:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=7216s) [2:01:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=7260s).
- X-axis could likely be stretched to roughly 400mm before becoming a problem (a rough, untested estimate), mainly limited by the CASA enclosure size — for genuinely bigger work, the advice is to wait for "Atlas," a larger dual-table machine in development (main table ~350×400mm plus a separate ~350×350×200mm accessories table sharing one X-axis) [2:21:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=8507s) [2:24:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=8668s) [2:26:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=8774s).
- The bundled spindle is 1.5kW but housed in an 80mm body (rather than the usual 65mm for that power) because it uses proper angular-contact bearings rather than cheaper ones [1:39:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=5966s) [1:39:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=5989s).
- Coolant advice: Milo cuts most materials fine dry or with just an air blast; mist coolant is called out as a real health hazard without proper extraction in an enclosed room, and isopropyl-alcohol coolant should never be used on ferrous metals (steel) due to spark/fire risk, though it's fine and standard for aluminum [1:41:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=6079s) [1:42:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=6161s).
- Two tool-changer approaches are in development: a cheap collet-based ATC (~$100 for 8 tools, using an extra spindle nut + collet + printed parts) versus a full pneumatic ATC (~£3,000+ for 8 tool holders); a 4th axis and vacuum table are also in progress [1:23:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=5031s).
- An upcoming split-jaw "Bulldog" vise was previewed on camera; feedback given to LDO is to make the jaws steel (not aluminum) for durability and to leave the base jaw unanodized to preserve flatness [2:11:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=7885s) [2:12:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=7974s).
- Roughly 500 Milo/Miley kits have been sold worldwide, with about 200 officially serialized and another ~50 known but unserialized; V2 factory kits aren't in production yet, with pre-orders possibly opening around December, though self-sourced V2 builds are already happening [1:26:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=5213s) [1:27:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=5251s).

## Details worth keeping
- Standard fastener sizes used throughout the design: M3×40, M3×10, M5×10, M5×12 — if unsure what screw goes somewhere, it's probably one of these [18:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=1098s).
- Heat insert convention: CNC Kitchen-style inserts are 4mm diameter × 4mm deep; Voron-spec inserts are 4.7mm diameter × 5mm deep. The designer models parts for CNC Kitchen size first, then converts to Voron spec [1:30:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=5418s) [1:31:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=5467s).
- When disassembling linear rails, mark each rail and its own carriages with matching letters (e.g., R/R1/R2) before removing carriages — preload is matched per-rail, so mixing carriages between rails is worse than mixing carriage order on the same rail [41:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=2499s) [47:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=2838s).
- Raw extrusion alone for a full Milo prices out at roughly £1,500+; getting CNC parts pre-tapped from a shop is notably more expensive than ordering the (free) manufacturing files and hand-tapping the holes yourself [2:29:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=8994s) [2:30:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=9019s).
- Milo's "Thor's Hammer" corner assembly uses a mix of screw lengths on purpose: the two center screws are blind joints holding the box assembly together permanently, while the outer screws are the ones you remove to detach the Z-axis from the Y-axis [1:18:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=4701s) [1:20:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVEFIVsAhIg&t=4813s).

## Skip it if
You're not building or planning to build an LDO Milo/Miley V2 kit — most of the runtime is unstructured stream banter, Discord/audio troubleshooting, and community small talk rather than a self-contained CNC tutorial.
