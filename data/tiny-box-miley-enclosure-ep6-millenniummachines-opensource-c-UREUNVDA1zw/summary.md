# Summary: Tiny Box Miley Enclosure Ep6 #millenniummachines #opensource #cnc

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw

---

## TL;DR
This is a casual livestream by the lead designer of the Milo CNC machine project (with his dog Brisket underfoot) working on the enclosure ("Casita") for Milo V2: installing a door, magnets, weatherstripping, and DIN-rail electronics mounts, while chatting with viewers and a Prusa rep (Tommy) about 3D printing and CNC design philosophy. Along the way he shares concrete engineering decisions — door-interlock behavior (pause vs. e-stop), a Nevermore filter VOC test, ABS vs. glass-filled PETG tradeoffs, and the difference between "Atlas" and "Fatlas" (two future CNC machine concepts) — plus a firm promise that Milo will stay backward-compatible through future versions. It's low-density, high-chatter content: useful for people already following the Milo/Atlas CNC project or interested in enclosure/electronics build details, not a tutorial for a general audience.

## Key points
- Milo V2 parts were expected to start shipping to vendors in about a week (early June), with pre-sales targeted for end of June, and the designer planned to build a kit himself before pre-sales open [7:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=464s).
- Door interlock design decision: the team (and chat) agreed a triggered door sensor should **pause** the CNC job rather than hard e-stop, though he flagged a real risk — an accidental "resume" press on the pause screen — as a reason e-stop might be safer after all [1:23:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=5038s) [1:26:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=5163s).
- General enclosure safety rule: the door should always open in the opposite direction from the e-stop location, so you can always reach the e-stop if you end up too close to the machine while it's running [1:58:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=7116s) [1:59:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=7184s).
- He measured actual VOC levels with a borrowed detector and found the Nevermore carbon filter barely reduces VOCs inside the chamber and does even less outside it (even with fresh carbon), though ambient VOC dissipation in a normal room is apparently harmless regardless [1:01:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=3683s) [1:01:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=3701s).
- Double-paneling an enclosure for soundproofing isn't worth it — sealing air leaks and isolating the machine from the floor/table (e.g., washing-machine mats) matters far more than adding a second panel layer [1:00:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=3604s).
- Atlas (his next CNC design, router-style) and "Fatlas" (a colleague's fixed-gantry take on the same original concept) are diverging on purpose, not out of conflict: Atlas targets ~£2,000–2,500 in materials, Fatlas closer to £5,000–6,000, putting it near professional-machine territory [40:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=2434s) [46:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=2767s).
- Milo V1.5 vs V2 are described as "not that different," and Milo has a track record of never breaking for him personally, though he distrusts relying on other community members' Milo builds since tolerances vary wildly (some hold 0.1mm, some don't) [19:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=1187s) [22:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=1324s).
- Firm commitment for the future: any Milo V3 will keep the same frame and linear rails for at least 2–3 minor iterations, explicitly to avoid repeating the V1.5→V2 backlash where early adopters couldn't upgrade [2:12:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=7929s) [2:13:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=7996s).
- Prusa's Core One L ships with a "Satin" bed sheet (a middle ground between full PEI and fully textured) because the L variant targets more engineering-grade filaments, while the standard Core One ships with PEI [23:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=1427s) [25:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=1511s).
- For CNC tool changers, "Four Pack" is the simpler/cheaper option good for standard 4-tool jobs (rougher, finisher, chamfer, one specialty tool), while "Spice Rack" is for more complex multi-tool setups [1:39:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=5945s) [1:40:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=6015s).
- He's souring on PETG-GF (glass-filled PETG), citing constant moisture absorption requiring drying before every print session, and says he'll likely never use it again despite giving it repeated chances [2:32:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=9153s).
- LDO's Voron/Milo electronics kits use dual-channel terminal blocks (live+neutral, not just live) for the MCB, which he calls marginally safer than the single-channel standard [2:15:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=8121s).

## Details worth keeping
- Atlas standard version will be either 500mm or 450mm in X, only a 150mm difference between variants [34:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=2087s).
- The team discovered Datron already uses "Next" as a name for their CNC firmware/software (also distinct from Tormach's PathPilot), so they may need to slightly rename their own in-development tool [36:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=2165s).
- Reason he lost use of a shipping-container workshop: he got permission, spent about £1,000 kitting it out, then was told the landlord's insurance didn't cover it after all [38:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=2326s).
- Idea floated on stream: build a 3D printer oriented on its side so the Y-axis (vertical build direction) aligns with gravity, which should improve bridging performance versus printing upside-down or right-side-up [56:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=3394s).
- He avoids modding the machines he personally develops (Milo, Atlas) so he can catch stock-configuration issues without confounding them with custom parts [00:20:39ish is reliability topic; modding-avoidance detail itself isn't separately timestamped but ties to the reliability discussion above].
- Practical caution: he had to redesign hinge-adjacent geometry live on stream after discovering a frame corner interferes with the hinge, meaning a printed hinge replacement wasn't possible as planned [55:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UREUNVDA1zw&t=3302s).

## Skip it if
You're not already following the Milo/Atlas CNC-machine project or don't care about enclosure/electronics build minutiae — this is an unscripted, chatty build-log stream with a lot of personal banter (including a long, unrelated tangent about a co-host's living situation) and offers little to a general 3D-printing or CNC audience.
